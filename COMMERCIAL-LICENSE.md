Kairos-Bridge AI Journal ("Kairos Journal, created by Steffan Douglas Haskins") is offered two ways:

1. **Free, open source, under the GNU AGPL-3.0-or-later** (see `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`). Anyone can use, study, change and share it, including commercially, **as long as** they follow the AGPL. That means sharing their source code under the same license when they distribute it, and offering it to users who reach a modified version over a network.
2. **Under a separate paid commercial license** from the copyright holder, for organizations that want to use it without the AGPL's obligations.

## Who needs a commercial license?

You need one if you want to **embed the journal in, or run it as part of, a closed-source product or service** without releasing your source code under the AGPL. Typical examples:

- shipping it inside proprietary software or a device;
- running a modified version as a hosted service (for example, a memory feature inside an AI assistant) without offering users its source code;
- combining it with code you can't or won't license under the AGPL;
- needing contract terms the AGPL doesn't provide (such as warranties, indemnities or support commitments).

You **don't** need one if you're happy to follow the AGPL, for example for personal use, research, or a product whose source you publish under the AGPL.

## What a commercial license would grant

Subject to the signed agreement, a commercial license is meant to grant:

- **use without the AGPL copyleft obligations**: the right to use, modify, embed and distribute the software, or run it as a service, in closed-source form, within the agreed scope;
- **negotiated attribution**: how, and whether, "Kairos Journal, created by Steffan Douglas Haskins" must be shown;
- **negotiated terms**: field of use, number of users or deployments, duration, territory, updates, support, and any warranties or indemnities.

## Patent-pending technology

The journal practices technology that is **patent pending (U.S. Provisional Application 63/912,083)**. A commercial license is intended to include, alongside the copyright license, a license under the related patent rights for the licensed use, as set out in the signed agreement.

(Separately, section 11 of the AGPL grants recipients of the AGPL-licensed code a patent license for the claims that code practices. A commercial license covers use outside the AGPL's terms.)
